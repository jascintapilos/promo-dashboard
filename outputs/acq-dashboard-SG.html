The proof — one live holdout test

turns a strong reading into proof · recommendation #3

Every grade here is directional — it compares a bonus to a player's own past or to look-alike players, which regression-to-the-mean, seasonality and would-deposit-anyway habit all inflate. The one clean separator is a randomised withhold.

What the test is

Pick one promo. Withhold it from a random half of eligible players (Control); offer it as normal to the other half (Full). Everything else stays identical. After 60–90 days, compare the two halves.

How it reads — KEEP or STOP

KEEP if the offered half earns more net revenue than the withheld half — it covered its own bonus cost (break-even is zero; our numbers are already after the bonus). STOP if the offered half is no better. A wide result is inconclusive, not a default cut.

The design, in plain terms

Randomise per player, and keep every other promo live in both halves — so it answers "this offer vs not, in the real world where substitutes exist". A clean per-code effect that cancels the rebound-anyway noise.

The bigger prize

Test a ladder of ~12–15 promos across the range and check whether the cheap directional number can be trusted to grade the ~150 we never test. If it can, a handful of tests governs the whole book.

Guardrails

Screen out at-risk / high-value players before randomising — never withhold from someone we're trying to save. A hard block stops anyone handing the withheld offer to a Control player. One code at a time — the results don't add up, so don't batch-cut.

Honest limits

It proves KEEP vs STOP for the tested promo — not how big to make it, and it can't split the bonus money from the reminder (a later, separate test). Runs as two cycles under one pre-agreed plan.

◆
The ask (recommendation #3): run one Lane A matched holdout first, and hold a NO-GO on the remaining big cuts until the read-out. That's what turns "a strong reading" into a number you can bank.

Notes

  • Cost per new depositor = new-player bonus spend ÷ new depositors. Bonus paid to existing players (deposit bonuses) is not charged to winning new depositors — otherwise a mostly deposit-bonus "welcome" looks falsely expensive.
  • New % = share of takers who were genuinely new (no prior deposit). Below 40%, the code is really a deposit bonus for existing players and is routed to the Retention tab, not graded here.
  • Week-1 net revenue = the profit kept in the first 7 days, shown as context. Real new-player offers often run this negative by design; a strongly positive figure usually means the code reached existing players (check New %).
  • Came back in 30 days = share of new depositors who came back within 30 days. The (x/y) is how many of the new depositors have had the full 30 days pass — a faded % rests on a minority and is still settling.
  • This ranks bonuses against each other, not against running no bonus. Proving the bonus adds extra needs a proper controlled test (recommended next).
  • Base = people who used the bonus (redeemed/active); figures cover 1 Jan–25 Aug, data to 26 Aug. August is a partial month (dashed on the trend); the 30-day came-back figure uses claims where the full 30 days has passed (to 28 Jul).
