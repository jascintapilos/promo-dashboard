One row per bonus. The call follows from cost per new depositor and 30-day stickiness. New % = share of takers who were genuinely new; below 40% the code is a reload, judged in Retention.

Notes

  • Cost per new depositor = new-player bonus spend ÷ new depositors. Bonus paid to existing players (reloads) is not charged to acquisition — otherwise a mostly-reload "welcome" looks falsely expensive.
  • New % = share of takers who were genuinely new (no prior deposit). Below 40%, the code is really a reload for existing players and is routed to the Retention tab, not graded here.
  • Wk-1 NGR = net revenue in the first 7 days, shown as context. Real acquisition offers often run this negative by design; a strongly positive figure usually means the code reached existing players (check New %).
  • 30-day stick = share of new depositors who came back within 30 days. The (x/y) is how many of the FTDs have a matured 30-day window — a faded % rests on a minority and is still settling.
  • This ranks bonuses against each other, not against running no bonus. True incrementality needs a holdout test (recommended next).
  • Base = people who used the bonus (redeemed/active); figures cover 1 Jan–25 Aug, data to 26 Aug. August is a partial month (dashed on the trend); 30-day stick uses matured claims (to 28 Jul).

Decision rules

Money is the judge here: NGR Lift per RM is the extra net revenue a bonus keeps (after its own cost) for every RM spent. "Retains" = deposits again above what that player type normally would.
